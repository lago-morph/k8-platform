# agent instruction

**Verify reachability via CI before assuming the sandbox can reach a cluster service.** "When a handoff or runbook says a cluster service (ArgoCD, the kube-API, any in-cluster endpoint) is reachable 'directly from the sandbox', do NOT rely on it until you have probed it from THIS sandbox. Sandbox egress is environment-specific and may be proxied: a clean TLS handshake followed by a 503/403 means you reached a proxy, not the service. If the sandbox cannot reach it but CI can (confirm with a read-only `verify` dispatch), drive the action from CI — where the AWS/state creds also live (§10.1) — not from the sandbox. State the strength of the claim: 'CI verify saw HTTP 200' is an observation; 'the sandbox is blocked' is an exclusion proven by the 503-with-clean-TLS."

*Grounded in: auto-007 — the handoff claimed "the sandbox has permissive egress, call ArgoCD directly"; this sandbox 503'd ArgoCD on every path while a CI `management verify` proved ArgoCD was HTTP-200, blocking the platform-cluster sync for the rest of the run.*

# justification

A prior session's handoff baked in "ArgoCD is internet-facing and reachable directly from the sandbox; don't hunt for a sync workflow." That was true in that session's sandbox and false in auto-007's. Acting on the stale claim cost a multi-step detour: ten minutes of polling a 503, a wrong leading hypothesis that argocd-server was crashlooping, and an attempt to build a CI sync workflow that then hit a second blocker — when one read-only `management verify` dispatch up front would have shown ArgoCD was healthy and the problem was sandbox egress. The marginal cost of the rule is one cheap probe (a curl, or a `verify` dispatch) before trusting a "reachable from the sandbox" claim; the cost of skipping it is debugging a phantom outage and mis-attributing an environmental boundary to a service failure. Reachability is a per-sandbox property, not a durable fact — treat any committed claim about it as a hypothesis to re-test.
