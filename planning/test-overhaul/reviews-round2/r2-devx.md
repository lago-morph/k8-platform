# Round-2 Adversarial Review — DevX & Maintainability

**Reviewer persona:** developer-experience & maintainability adversary.
**Target:** `planning/test-overhaul/synthesis/SYNTHESIZED-PLAN.md` (merge of PLAN-{A,B,C} + 9 round-1 reviews).
**One question I keep asking:** will engineers actually run and maintain this, or will it rot / get quietly disabled within a few sessions?

Verdict up front: the synthesis is a genuine improvement over the three source plans. It killed the worst DevX offenders (six-tier taxonomy, hand-maintained manifest, probe-SA fantasy, stranded `live-verify` workflow) and it grounds nearly every claim in a real file. But the document has grown to **657 lines of dense prose with ~70 inline review-citation tags**, and that is itself the headline maintainability risk: *the plan is now harder to onboard to than the test suite it replaces.* Several "resolved" criticals are resolved **on paper** — the mechanism is named but its hardest implementation detail (the one that decides whether it works or mis-fires and blocks everyone) is deferred to "the plan-to-implementation handoff." Below, ranked.

---

## CRITICAL

### C1. The DERIVED coverage manifest will mis-parse these compositions — and a mis-parsing gate blocks *every* PR, not just the under-covered one
This is the single finding I most want finalization to not wave through. §3.5 and the blocker matrix make `test_live_coverage.sh` (PRE-FLIGHT, every push) the load-bearing anti-rot mechanism: derive the MR-kind set from `crossplane/**` `resources[].base.kind` + the action set from `irsa.tf`, fail red if any derived kind/action lacks a referenced live test. The plan asserts this is feasible by pointing at `test_iam_required_actions.sh`. I read both the existing test and the actual compositions. The derivation is materially harder than the plan admits:

1. **`base.kind` is not at `spec.resources[]`.** Every composition is **pipeline mode** (`function-patch-and-transform`); the MR kinds live at `spec.pipeline[].input.resources[].base.kind`, nested ~7 levels deep, and the `function-environment-configs` step has its own `input.kind: Input` that is **not** an MR. A naive `grep kind:` or `yq '.spec.resources[]'` derivation gets the wrong set on day one.
2. **`base.kind` is interleaved with `providerConfigRef.kind: ClusterProviderConfig`** at varying indentation in *every* resource block (verified in all four comps). A line-oriented or insufficiently-anchored parser will enumerate `ClusterProviderConfig` and `ClusterSecretStore` as "provisioned MR kinds" and demand live tests for them — tests that can never exist. That is a **false-positive that reds every push** until someone reverse-engineers the parser.
3. **Ambiguous short kinds.** `Secret`, `ExternalSecret`, `Record`, `Certificate`, `Role`, `Instance` appear as bare `kind:` with the real identity only in `apiVersion` (`iam.aws.m.upbound.io`, `rds.aws.m.upbound.io`, `kubernetes`/ESO groups). The coverage registry must key on **group+kind**, not kind, or it conflates the ESO `ExternalSecret` with anything else named similarly.
4. **Conditional resources.** `xspokeaccess.yaml` has 19 hits for condition/patch logic; resources can be present-but-patched-out. The derivation counts a kind as "provisioned" that a given XR may never instantiate — so the BRING-UP `expect-full` promotion (§3.3) keyed off it will FAIL on a legitimately-absent conditional resource.

**Why this is Critical and not Major:** the failure mode is not "coverage gap slips through." It is **the gate mis-fires and blocks unrelated PRs**, which is the *exact* trigger for "someone adds `# coverage-skip` / comments out the test under deadline" — the rot the plan exists to prevent, now self-inflicted. A coverage gate that cries wolf gets disabled faster than no gate at all.

**Fix (must land in finalization, not handoff):**
- Specify the derivation as `yq` over the **explicit path** `.spec.pipeline[] | select(.step==...) | .input.resources[].base | [.apiVersion, .kind]`, with `ClusterProviderConfig`/`ClusterSecretStore`/the `function-*` `Input` kinds on a named, tested exclude-list. Pin it with a **fixture test of the deriver itself** (feed it the four committed comps, assert the exact expected MR-group-kind set) so a composition-format change reds the *deriver's own* test, not 40 downstream PRs.
- Key the registry on `group/kind`, never bare kind.
- For conditional resources, the registry entry must carry the condition (or be in the `extra:` allowlist), and `expect-full` must consult it. State the rule: a conditional MR is `expect-full` only when its gating param is set in the applied XR.
- Add a residual-risk acceptance: until the deriver's fixture test exists, the coverage gate ships **warn-only** (prints, exits 0). §14.3 already half-admits derivation completeness is shaky; make the rollout reflect it.

### C2. "Coupled to the change" still has a hole the size of a new MR kind — and the plan's own §6.2 red-first ritual is the only thing plugging it
The synthesis resolves round-1's C1 (coupling-is-goodwill) by making *absence of coverage* CI-red via the derived manifest. Good — **if C1 above is solved.** But there is a second leak the merge introduced and did not close: the gate proves "every derived MR kind has a *referenced* test"; it does **not** prove the referenced test *exercises the new behavior*. §3.5 tries to patch this ("must require a real referenced check, not merely a tier declaration"), but the check it specifies is structural (`cheap`→a BRING-UP check keyed to the kind exists). A developer under deadline adds the new MR kind, points the registry at an **existing, passing, adjacent** BRING-UP test (or a stub that asserts `true`), and the gate goes green. The coupling is back to goodwill, just with one more hoop.

This is the auto-012 failure restated: the discipline was *written down* (AGENTS §6.1/§6.2/§6.4) and 8 blockers shipped anyway. The plan leans hard on §6.2 (each test must FAIL red against the pre-fix state) and §6.4 (adversarial subagent) to give coupling teeth — but **those are process disciplines, identical in kind to the ones that already failed.** The plan does not add a *mechanical* "this test actually touches this kind" check.

**Fix:** the mapping test should assert the referenced BRING-UP check, when run in `--dry-run`/recorded-fixture mode (§9), **names the registered group/kind in its apply payload** (grep the test's fixture for the kind it claims to defend). Not perfect, but it converts "any green test counts" into "a test that at least instantiates this kind counts" — a mechanical floor under the goodwill. State plainly in §3.5 that beyond this floor, behavioral coupling rests on §6.2/§6.4, which are the mechanisms that *previously failed*, and that the derived-gate is the only net-new mechanical teeth.

### C3. The executed-test FLOOR / `expect-full` is the most fragile load-bearing part and its correctness rests on a hand-maintained phase→resource map
§3.3 is the anti-silent-regression core, and §14.4 honestly flags it: the whole floor depends on the bring-up "correctly declaring which phase it applied" so `expect-full` is set for the right resources. Two problems the persona cares about:

1. **Where does the phase→expected-resource map live?** The plan never says. If it's a second hand-maintained table, it drifts exactly like the rejected `COST_TIERS.yaml`; if it's derived, it inherits C1's parsing fragility. This is unspecified and it is the hinge of requirements 1/2/6.
2. **The three skip states (`not-applicable` / `phase-not-applied` / `precondition-absent-but-expected-present`) are a real cognitive load on every future test author.** Getting the classification wrong in *either* direction is silent: mark something `phase-not-applied` that should be `expect-full` → blocker #5 reappears (the thing this exists to stop); mark `expect-full` something genuinely conditional → red on a legitimate skip → disable pressure. The `phase=test` unit tests (§3.3) test the *orchestrator's tabulation*, not whether each real test chose the right skip state — that classification is per-test goodwill again.

**Fix:** (a) Specify the phase→resource expectation as **derived from the same registry** as C1 (one source of truth), with the phase tag on the registry entry, so "what phase builds kind X" is not a second map. (b) Make the default **fail-closed**: a BRING-UP test that does not explicitly declare its skip-state class is treated as `expect-full` (so the unsafe default is "loud," not "silent-green"). (c) Add a meta-test: for each registry kind, assert exactly one skip-state classifier is wired, so an unclassified new kind reds at push.

## MAJOR

### M1. The plan document itself is now an onboarding tax — and a single-author artifact nobody else can safely edit
657 lines, ~70 `(reviewer-XN)` citation tags inline, 12 dense sections plus a rejected-list and an open-questions list. The citations are audit-useful for *this* review round but are noise for the engineer who has to *implement* from it. More to the point: there is no **acceptance checklist** — a flat "these N things must be true for P1/P2/.../done" list that an implementer can tick and a reviewer can verify. §10's matrix calls itself "the acceptance criterion" but it's only the blocker→assertion mapping, not the mechanism acceptance (derived-gate works, floor reds on all-skip, register cap enforced, redaction filter present). Without that checklist the plan will be implemented selectively — the cheap static parts (P1) land, the behavioral teeth (P3/P4 reaper, isolation, floor) get deferred "to a follow-up" and never arrive, and the suite ships *looking* complete while missing exactly the parts that give it teeth.
**Fix:** add a §15 "Done means" checklist: one line per non-negotiable mechanism, each mapping to the test that proves *the mechanism itself* works (most already exist as `phase=test` unit tests scattered in the prose — collect them). Move the inline citation tags to footnotes or strip them at finalization; they do not survive into an implementable spec.

### M2. The fast inner loop (§9) is asserted but its hardest half — a credible fake cloud for these MR kinds — is hand-waved
§9 (resolves round-1 C2, the flake-fatigue → disable pathway, which I rate the highest-probability rot vector) mandates every BRING-UP test's *logic* run sandbox-local <60s on kind + moto/localstack **or** a `run.sh --dry-run` against recorded fixtures. The `--dry-run`/recorded-fixture path is credible and cheap. The moto/localstack path is **not** credible for this repo's MR kinds: these are **Upbound `*.aws.m.upbound.io` provider** resources reconciled by the provider controller against AWS; moto/localstack do not speak the Crossplane provider protocol, and the provider's view of "the cloud" is not moto. So "kind + fake cloud" effectively collapses to "kind + the existing fake-cloud chainsaw stub" — which the plan *itself rejects* (§13 T2: "a green that means nothing is worse than no test"). The only honest fast loop here is the `--dry-run`-against-recorded-fixtures one.
**Fix:** drop the moto/localstack alternative from §9; commit to **`--dry-run` against recorded provider/AWS-response fixtures** as *the* fast loop, and specify where the fixtures live and how they're refreshed (else they rot and the dry-run validates against a stale shape). This is the difference between "we have a fast loop" and "we said we'd have one." Without a real fast loop, §11's false-fail SLO and §3.2's disable switch combine into the exact escape valve under deadline.

### M3. Enforcement-via-`tests/unit/run.sh` is real teeth, but the SKIP_BASELINE diff-gate has a known evasion and a maintenance cost the plan undersells
§3.4 routes the gate through `tests/unit/run.sh` (correct — the env can't touch workflows, and §6.16 sync is cited). But:
- **The baseline ratchet is gameable and noisy.** "Fail if skip-count-on-HEAD rose vs committed `SKIP_BASELINE` without a matching `SKIP_REGISTER.yaml` diff" means *every* legitimately-skipped run (hub-only bring-up: 6/8 spoke checks skip) must either be `phase-not-applied` (not counted) or registered. The line between "count" and "don't count" is C3's classification again. And a developer can lower the baseline number to match a new skip and the gate goes green — the baseline file is hand-edited by definition. The plan needs the baseline to be **derived/asserted**, not a trust-me integer.
- **`expires` escalation (warn→fail after grace) is good** (it stops a calendar event redding unrelated PRs — the round-1 DevX-B M1 fix), but combined with the register **cap** (fail if >N entries) you get a cliff: N-1 entries fine, the Nth legitimate disable reds the whole repo for everyone. Set N with headroom and make the cap message name *which* entries to retire, or it's just another wall people climb over by deleting someone else's entry.
**Fix:** derive the "expected skip set" from the registry+phase (C3), compare the *set* not a count, so the gate is "you skipped something not in the registry," not "the integer went up." Specify N and the grace window *in the plan* (§14.7 punts them to handoff — the two values that decide whether the gate is livable should not be TBD).

### M4. Per-resource live tests + fixtures + redaction + reaper = a large standing maintenance surface, and the plan never sizes it
§5 says cheap MR kinds get **BOTH** AFTER-THE-FACT and BRING-UP (generalize `11_platform_secret_e2e.sh` "to each XRD"). That 142-line script is one of the more careful files in the repo, and it's *one* XRD. Generalizing its shape (apply → wait → cloud-exists → behaves → rotate → delete → assert-torn-down, plus run-id namespacing, plus synthetic-secret/redaction, plus reaper registration, plus the three skip-states) to every current and *future* XRD is a real per-kind tax — and §6.1 of AGENTS already mandates maximal coverage, so this compounds. The plan's §11 false-fail SLO and §11 quarantine are themselves *more* machinery to maintain (an append-only outcome log whose storage §14.6 admits is unresolved on ephemeral accounts).
**Fix:** provide a **single parametrized BRING-UP harness** (one library, kind+name+expected-cloud-assertion+teardown-selector as inputs) so adding an XRD is a fixture+registry entry, not a new 142-line script. The plan gestures at "generalize the shape" — make it a hard requirement that there is *one* harness, not N copies, and that the per-kind delta is data. This is the difference between a suite engineers extend and one they route around. Also resolve §14.6 (outcome-log storage) before P6 is even sketched, or the SLO/quarantine machinery is built on sand.

## MINOR

### m1. `route53:*`/`eks:*` ceiling-lint annotations will themselves drift
§4.1 requires inline `# lpe-justified:` annotations on each wildcard, parsed by the test. I confirmed `eks:*` + seven `Resource="*"` blocks exist today. Annotation-parsing-as-contract is fine, but annotations rot (someone copies a block, the annotation no longer matches the action). Require the annotation to *name the actions/reason it covers* and have the test assert the annotated scope still matches the block — else it's a comment the test trusts blindly.

### m2. "Honesty rename" (§1: lints not a directory move) is the right call, but the README+tag will diverge from reality
Relabeling `tests/unit/` conceptually-as-lints via a README+tag (not a move) avoids the ~60-file blast radius — correct, well-reasoned. But a README that says "these are lints" while the directory stays `tests/unit/` and new files keep landing there as `test_*.sh` will read as stale within a few sessions. Minor because it's cosmetic, but add a one-line lint that new `tests/unit/test_*.sh` carry the `lint` tag so the conceptual claim stays true.

### m3. §6.2 red-first against "the pre-fix state" is impossible for some blockers once the fix is merged
The plan repeatedly invokes §6.2 (ship each test red-first against the unfixed code). For blockers already fixed in `irsa.tf` (the `rds:*`, `iam:Tag*` grants are *present* now — verified), there is no unfixed state to go red against without reverting the grant. The plan acknowledges this for the *live* negative (§2.3) but still says P1–P5 each "ships red-first against the pre-fix state." For the already-fixed blockers that's a no-op or requires a throwaway revert branch. State explicitly which blockers can be red-first (future regressions) vs which get a one-time "remove the grant locally, confirm `simulate-principal-policy` flips to deny, restore" demonstration, so an implementer doesn't burn a loop discovering the contradiction.

### m4. The plan creates `tests/live/` as a new top-level surface parallel to `tests/integration/` — and the relationship is muddy
§3.1 introduces `tests/live/run.sh` with inverted skip semantics, while §13 keeps `tests/integration/` as "the real-cluster base" and §12-P2 says "promote the existing integration probes into `tests/live/`." So integration tests get *moved/copied* into a new tree with different skip semantics. That's a migration with its own drift risk (two runners, two skip conventions, the `01–13` scripts half-migrated). Clarify: is `tests/integration/` deleted after promotion, or do both persist? Two live runners with opposite skip semantics is a footgun for the next author who copies the wrong template.

## What finalization MUST NOT weaken

**The center holds — keep it:** drive-the-real-controller-under-IRSA (no probe SA, §2 NON-GOAL), `simulate-principal-policy` for the expensive/already-granted permission classes, on-by-default with all-skipped-reads-red, and reaper-runs-first remediate-not-report. These are correct and hard-won; do not trade them for simplicity.

**But the one thing finalization must not lose is C1's corollary:** the anti-rot teeth (derived coverage gate + executed-floor + `expect-full`) are only as good as the **derivation actually parsing these pipeline-mode compositions correctly**, and that derivation must ship **with a fixture test of the deriver itself and in warn-only mode until that test is green.** A derived gate that mis-fires is *worse* than the hand-maintained manifest it replaced, because it blocks everyone and trains the team to bypass coverage checks. If finalization keeps the elegant "derived, not hand-maintained" claim but punts the parser robustness to "the handoff," the plan's central maintainability win becomes its central maintainability liability. Resolve the parser, key on group/kind, fixture-test the deriver, ship warn-first — or the whole §3 floor is goodwill wearing a gate's clothes.
