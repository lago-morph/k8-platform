# 6.29 Verify a constraint against the repo or owner before encoding it into subagent briefs

**Verify a constraint against the repo or owner before encoding it into
subagent briefs.** Before baking a constraint or premise into briefs that fan
out to many subagents or many review rounds, verify it against the repo and (if
it is owner-policy) the owner; a false premise propagates across every
downstream agent and round and is expensive to unwind.

*Grounded in: 2026-06-07 — the false "can't edit workflows" premise (and the
CI-as-trigger framing) seeded 3 source plans, 14 reviews, and the synthesis
before two owner corrections unwound it.*

## Justification

I encoded "this environment can't edit `.github/workflows/*`" into the
synthesized plan and the round-2/round-3 reviewer briefs. It was false —
jentic's Contents-PUT edits workflow files, a fact established earlier in the
very same session — and the owner had to correct it; a second false framing
("the build isn't CI") followed close behind. By the time the corrections
landed, the false premise had propagated through three independently-authored
source plans, fourteen adversarial reviews, and a full synthesis, forcing two
written correction notes and two revision passes. The marginal cost of
verifying a premise once — a `grep`, an `ls`, or a single question to the owner
— is trivial; the cost of a wrong premise inside a fan-out is multiplied by
every agent and every round downstream of it. Treat any load-bearing constraint
that will be copied into multiple briefs as something to confirm before, not
after, the fan-out.
